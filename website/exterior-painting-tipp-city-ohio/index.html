Why Us

Most Paint Jobs Fail at the Prep, Not the Paint

We got into painting from the other direction. Years of washing Miami Valley houses means we've seen the underside of hundreds of failed paint jobs, and they nearly all failed for the same three reasons.

1. It was painted over chalk

Aluminum and older vinyl siding oxidize into a fine powder. Paint applied over that powder is bonded to the powder, not the siding, and it comes off in sheets in two or three winters. Chalked surfaces have to be washed down to sound material and primed with a bonding primer. This is the single most common failure on 1960s and 70s ranches around Vandalia, Piqua and Huber Heights.

2. It was painted over algae

Green algae and black staining are living organisms. Seal them under a coat of paint and they keep growing, lifting the film from underneath and blooming back through it within a season. A wall has to be genuinely killed and cleaned — not just rinsed — before it's painted. That's a soft wash, and it's included.

3. It was painted in the wrong weather

Ohio's exterior season runs roughly May through early October. Most coatings need surface and air temperature above about 50°F, and the overnight low matters as much as the daytime high — paint applied at 65°F that drops to 38°F before it cures will not perform, no matter what it cost. We'd rather move your date than paint into weather that guarantees a callback.

Pre-1978 homes and lead paint

Older homes in Tipp City, Troy, Piqua and Dayton's historic neighborhoods may have lead-based paint under the current coats. That changes the prep — controlled, contained work rather than dry sanding or blasting that puts dust into your yard. If we find it or suspect it, you'll hear about it before we start, along with what it means for the schedule and the price.

How It Runs

Wash. Prep. Prime. Paint.

1

Wash

A full soft wash of every surface getting coated. Algae killed, chalk removed, dirt gone.

2

Dry & assess

We let it dry properly and then walk it with you — what's sound, what's failing, what needs replacing before paint.

3

Prep

Scrape, sand where appropriate, caulk the joints, mask the glass and cover the landscaping.

4

Prime

Bare wood, chalked surfaces and stain-prone areas get primed. Skipping this is why coats fail.

5

Paint & walk it

Coated, cleaned up, and walked with you before we load out. Anything you spot gets fixed on the spot.
