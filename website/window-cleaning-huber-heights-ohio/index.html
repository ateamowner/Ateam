Local Knowledge

Identical Houses, Thirty-Six Years of Different Windows

Huber Homes built here from 1956 to 1992. The brick openings are the same street to street; what has been put in them since is not.

Almost every house has had windows replaced at some point, and they went in at different times, by different installers, to whatever standard was normal that decade. So two houses that look identical from the road can be completely different jobs: one has modern tilt-in vinyl double-hung, the next still has original aluminum with a triple-track storm over the top, and the one after that has a mix because it was done room by room.

That is why we ask what you actually have before quoting rather than counting openings from the curb. It is also why a neighbor's price is not automatically your price, even on a street where every front door is in the same place.

The Brick Problem

The Stain Under the Sill Isn't the Window's Fault

On a brick house, look under the windows rather than at them. There is usually a darker trail running down the mortar below each opening.

That is runoff. Rain hits the glass, picks up whatever is sitting on it, crosses the sill and runs down the wall. Brick sheds it; the mortar joints are porous and hold it. Over thirty or fifty years that trail sets into the joint and becomes the thing people actually notice from the street, long before they notice the glass.

Cleaning the windows and leaving the wall means the trail starts rebuilding immediately, and the contrast makes it more obvious than before. If the mortar below is stained, doing the glass and a soft wash on the same visit is the version that actually looks finished — and bundling takes 12% off.

Why It Spots

Hard Water Is the Real Enemy

Miami Valley water comes up through limestone, which loads it with dissolved calcium and magnesium. Wash a window with it and the water evaporates while the minerals stay put, as the white haze people read as "still dirty".

It is why a careful job with a bucket and a hose can leave glass looking worse than when you started, and why south-facing panes are always the worst — they dry fastest, so the minerals set before anyone can wipe them off.

Exterior glass gets rinsed with purified water that has had those minerals stripped out. It dries leaving nothing at all, which is why we do not towel the outside. There is nothing to towel.
