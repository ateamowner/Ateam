Where Englewood Ends For Us

US-40 — the old National Road — still runs through the middle of town. I-70 sits on the north edge. That combination is why Englewood exteriors collect two different kinds of dirt at once.

Most of the Miami Valley is a biology problem: green algae and black streaks feeding on shade and moisture. Englewood has that too, especially on the north walls that sit toward Englewood MetroPark and the Stillwater River, where the air stays damp longer after a rain.

It also has a road-film problem. National Road and the interstate throw tire rubber and brake dust onto the walls and glass that face them. Rinse that with plain water and you mostly relocate it. It needs a detergent that lifts the oil so the rinse can carry it off.

We cover the whole city, plus Clayton, Union and Randolph Township around the edges. Nearby we are just as regular in Vandalia, Tipp City, Huber Heights and Dayton.
