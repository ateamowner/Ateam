Local Knowledge

Three Generations of Window in One Town

Fairborn's glass falls into three groups, and they are not the same job. Anyone quoting a flat per-window price without asking which you have is guessing.

Original wood sash on the older stock

Scattered through the older streets, including the houses that were physically moved over from Osborn in the 1920s. These have real putty holding the pane, and on an original sash that putty can be brittle or failing. The glass gets worked; the putty stays dry. If something is crumbling we tell you rather than finding out the hard way.

Aluminum triple-track storms on the base-boom streets

The 1950s and 60s neighborhoods are full of them. The track at the bottom collects grit, paint flakes and dead insects, and every time it rains that drains straight back down the pane. Clean the glass and leave the track and it looks dirty again within a fortnight, which is why people assume storms just get filthy fast. They do not; the channel is loading them.

Modern replacements on everything since

Most houses have had windows put in at some point, at different times and to whatever standard was normal that decade. Plenty tilt in, which sorts the inside. The outside still has to be reached.

Why It Spots

The Water Is the Problem, Not the Glass

Water here comes up through limestone, loaded with dissolved calcium and magnesium. Wash a window with it and the water goes but the minerals stay, as the white haze people read as still dirty.

It is why a careful job with a bucket and a hose can leave glass looking worse than before it was touched, and why south-facing panes come out worst — they dry fastest, so the minerals set before anyone can wipe them off.

Exterior glass gets rinsed with purified water that has had those minerals stripped out. It dries leaving nothing at all, which is why we do not towel the outside.
