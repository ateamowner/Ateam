Local Knowledge

The Whole Town's Flatwork Is Aging Together

Fairborn grew to roughly six times its size between 1950 and 1970. That is a lot of concrete poured inside one narrow window, and it is all hitting the same stage of life at the same time.

Walk a base-boom street and you can see it: the same surface wear, the same joints opening up, the same green-black film on the shaded halves. Nobody has let anything go. It is one cohort of concrete reaching sixty-odd years together.

It also explains why a single clean driveway stands out so much here. On a street where every slab is the same age and the same color, one that has been washed does not look slightly better — it looks like a different house.

What actually comes off, and what does not

The green-black film is biological and comes off completely. Tire marks and general road grime come off. Oil that has soaked into the pores needs a degreaser and dwell time, not more pressure, and how much lifts depends on how long it sat. Rust from a downspout or a planter needs a different chemical again. And if the slab has spalled, you are looking at exposed aggregate, which is the concrete itself — washing does not bring that back and anyone telling you otherwise is selling.

We will tell you which of those you have before we start rather than after.

Method

Surface Cleaner, Not a Wand

A pressure wand cleans in a narrow line, so covering a driveway with one means overlapping dozens of passes by eye. Miss the overlap and you get stripes, which show up worse in low sun than the dirt did.

A rotary surface cleaner is a shrouded head with bars that spin under it. It covers a wide band evenly at a consistent distance from the slab, so the finish is uniform edge to edge. It is slower to set up and much faster to run, and it is the difference between a clean driveway and a striped one.
