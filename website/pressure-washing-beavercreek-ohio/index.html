Local Knowledge

Three Kinds of Beavercreek Flatwork

Because the city grew subdivision by subdivision rather than outward from a center, the concrete here comes in distinct generations sitting side by side. The finish gives it away faster than the house does.

Older broom-finished slabs

Narrower drives, heavier broom texture, more joints. Sixty-odd years of freeze-thaw means some are spalling at the surface. They clean up well, but a heavy broom finish holds growth deeper in the texture and takes longer to bring back than a smooth slab.

The 80s and 90s corridors

Wider, smoother, fewer joints, and by now well shaded where the subdivision trees have matured. These are the classic call: a north-facing half that is green-black and a south-facing half that is nearly clean, on the same slab.

Stamped, sawn and paver work on the newer builds

This is where damage happens. Stamped concrete is usually sealed, and too much pressure lifts the sealer in patches, which looks considerably worse than the dirt. Paver patios lose their jointing sand if you go at them hard, which destabilises the field. Both want lower pressure, a shallower angle and more chemistry — and with pavers you should expect to re-sand afterwards. That is normal.

Method

Surface Cleaner, Not a Wand

A wand cleans in a narrow line, so covering a drive means overlapping dozens of passes by eye. Miss an overlap and you get stripes, which show up worse in low sun than the dirt ever did.

A rotary surface cleaner is a shrouded head with spinning bars that covers a wide band at a constant distance from the slab. Even finish, edge to edge. On stamped or sealed work we drop the pressure and let the detergent carry more of the job.
