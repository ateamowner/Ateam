Local Knowledge

Why Every House on Your Street Has the Same Black Streaks

Because they are the same roof. Charles Huber's company put up more than ten thousand houses here to a small number of plans, so a whole street often shares one elevation, one pitch and one orientation to the sun.

People take that personally. They assume they let the roof go, or that it is failing early. It is neither — it is thirty to seventy years of identical buildings weathering identically. The dark runs are Gloeocapsa magma, a cyanobacteria that feeds on the limestone filler in asphalt shingle and holds moisture against the surface.

That is also why booking with neighbors works better here than anywhere else we go. We can walk one house and know what the other nine look like. The saving comes back to you.

Brick walls below those roofs are a separate job. Mortar joints do not want pressure either — that is on the Huber Heights soft-washing page. This page is the shingles.
