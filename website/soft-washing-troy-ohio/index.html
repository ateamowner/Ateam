What It Actually Is

Chemistry, Not Force

Pressure washing cleans by hitting a surface hard enough to knock things off it. That works on concrete. On the side of a house it is the wrong tool, and in Troy it is the wrong tool on a lot of expensive houses.

Soft washing runs at about the pressure of a strong garden hose — roughly 100 to 500 PSI at the wall, against 1,500 to 4,000 for pressure washing. The cleaning is done by a biodegradable detergent that is left to dwell, then rinsed. The water is only there to carry the solution on and wash it back off.

The difference shows up months later. What you see growing on siding is a living organism, and its root structure sits down in the pores of the surface. Blast the top off and the wall looks clean on the day and green again by the following spring. Kill it and the wall stays clean until something new lands on it.

Local Knowledge

Why Troy Grows It Faster Than Most

Troy sits in the Great Miami river valley, and that shapes what happens to the outside of a house here more than most people expect.

The river holds humidity against the whole west side of town

The Great Miami runs along the north edge of downtown, with the levee and the trail beside it. Air over the valley floor stays damp longer than air a few miles inland, and the streets running down toward the water hold that moisture well into the morning. Two identical houses, one two blocks off the levee and one out on the east side, do not weather at the same rate. The river-side one goes first, every time.

Inside the 1807 boundary, force is the enemy

Troy's historic district traces the town limits from 1807, when the state made it the county seat: the river to the north, Clay Street east, Canal Street south, Oxford Street west. Inside that square you have American Foursquares, Dutch Colonials, Victorians and bungalows, most with painted wood somewhere in the mix and original trim worth keeping.

A pressure wand on painted clapboard forces water up behind the boards, where it has no way to dry. It comes back out through the paint as blistering the following summer, and the homeowner blames the paint. On a Foursquare with wide overhangs and deep porch detail there are a dozen places for water to get in and nowhere for it to leave. Everything inside that boundary gets soft washed, no exceptions.

The canopy is the other half of the problem

The established streets around the square and out along the river trail have mature trees over them, which is a large part of why people want to live there. It also means north and east walls sit in shade all day. Shade plus a damp valley is exactly the condition algae wants. When we walk a Troy house the north elevation is usually a full shade darker than the south, and that is where the work is.

Surface By Surface

What Gets Soft Washed

If force would damage it, it gets chemistry instead.

Vinyl and aluminum siding

The most common call in the post-war parts of town. Older aluminum chalks as it oxidises, and pressure grinds that chalk into streaks you cannot get back out. It needs a soak and a rinse.

Painted wood and clapboard

Foursquares, Victorians and bungalows near the square. Low pressure only, and we keep the solution off bare wood and away from window glazing putty.

Soft brick and lime mortar

Anything pre-war downtown. The mortar was made soft deliberately so the brick could move. Pressure scours it out of the joints and you do not get it back.

Asphalt shingle roofs

Black streaking is cyanobacteria, not dirt. Same low-pressure chemistry, no machine ever touching the shingle, no granule loss. Roof cleaning details →

Stucco, EIFS and dryvit

Turns up on the newer builds east of town. Pressure cracks the finish coat and water gets into the substrate behind it, which is an expensive mistake.

Decks, fences and pergolas

Cleaned without fuzzing the grain. If you are staining afterwards, the prep matters more than the stain. Read the deck guide →

What a Troy House Wash Costs

Published ranges, not a number invented on your driveway.

If you are having the driveway or walks done at the same time, that is $150–$300 on its own, and bundling the two is cheaper than booking them separately because we are already on site with the water hooked up.
