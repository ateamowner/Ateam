Local Knowledge

More Glass Per House Than Anywhere Else We Work

Beavercreek's newer subdivisions have more window area than any of our Miami County towns, and a lot of it is high up.

Two-story entries with a tall window over the door. Transoms above the front door and the garage. Great-room walls with stacked panes running up to a vaulted ceiling. Sunrooms and four-season rooms on the back. It looks superb and it is a genuinely bigger job than a ranch with eight openings at head height.

The entry glass is the one that matters most. It is the most visible window on the house, it is directly above where every visitor stands, and it is the one nobody has touched since they moved in — because it needs either a pole or a ladder and most people quite reasonably decide it is not worth it. That is included in what we quote, not added on the day when we discover it.

The older ranches are a different, easier job

Out on the postwar streets the glass is mostly at head height, often big picture windows and sliders. Access is easy, which keeps the price down, but the panes are large enough that any streak is obvious — which is where the purified-water rinse earns its keep.

Why It Spots

Hard Water, Not Dirty Glass

Water here comes up through limestone, so it carries dissolved calcium and magnesium. Wash a window with it and the water evaporates while the minerals stay put, as the white haze people read as "still dirty".

South-facing glass is always the worst because it dries fastest, so the minerals set before anyone can wipe them off. It is also why a careful DIY job with a bucket and hose can leave glass looking worse than before it was started.

Exterior glass gets rinsed with purified water that has had the minerals stripped out. Nothing is left to dry, which is why we do not towel the outside — there is nothing to towel.
