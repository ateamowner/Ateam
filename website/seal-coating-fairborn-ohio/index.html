Local Knowledge

Why So Many Fairborn Driveways Need This at Once

Fairborn's population boom between 1950 and 1970 poured an enormous amount of concrete in a short window. Fifty-plus years later, a lot of that concrete is reaching the point where surface scaling and pitting start showing up — and it's showing up on a lot of driveways around the same time.

Why one era of concrete ages predictably

Concrete from the same building boom tends to fail the same way at the same time, because it's the same mix, the same era's finishing techniques, and the same fifty-plus winters of freeze-thaw exposure. If your neighbor's driveway is starting to flake, yours from the same subdivision probably isn't far behind.

Sealing now is cheaper than replacing later

A full driveway replacement costs many times what sealing does. Catching concrete in the "starting to show wear" stage — rather than the "actively crumbling" stage — is exactly when a sealer does the most good.

Not every 1950s–70s driveway is a fit

If your driveway already has open cracks or sections heaving from tree roots, sealing slows future water damage but isn't a fix for what's already moved. We'll be straight with you about which category yours falls into before we quote it.
