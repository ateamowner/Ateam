Local Knowledge

Why Piqua's Climate Makes Sealing Worth It

Piqua grew up on the river and the canal, and the river is still setting the terms — humidity, shade and a mature tree canopy that keeps a lot of this town damp well into the afternoon. That's exactly the environment algae and moss like best.

Damp, shaded surfaces regrow algae fastest

Algae and moss need moisture to establish, and Piqua's tree cover and river humidity keep concrete and roofing damp longer than sunnier parts of Miami County. A wash removes what's there today; sealing closes off the porous surface algae spores would otherwise latch onto next.

It won't stop regrowth forever, but it slows it a lot

No sealer makes a shaded, humid surface immune to algae permanently. What it does is meaningfully extend the time between washes — often the difference between reappearing in one season versus two or three.

Pair it with your next wash, not a separate visit

Since sealer has to go on a clean, dry surface anyway, the cheapest way to add it is right after a scheduled wash rather than as its own trip. Ask about combined pricing when you book.
