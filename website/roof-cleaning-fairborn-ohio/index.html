Local Knowledge

The Base Boom Put a Whole Town's Roofs on the Same Clock

Fairborn is two villages that became one in 1950. Then the base did the rest. Street after street went up in those twenty years, which means street after street of the same shingle era, facing the same weather, streaking in the same decade.

That is a different problem than a mixed-age town. You are not looking at one neglected roof. You are looking at a cohort. North slopes go first because they stay wet longer. The chemistry is the same as anywhere we work: kill Gloeocapsa magma, leave the granules alone.

Scattered through the older parts of town are the moved Osborn houses — pre-1920 buildings on 1920s foundations. Those want extra care at the eaves and any original wood under the drip. The roof still gets a soft wash, not a wand.

We are based in Tipp City, so Fairborn is a real drive. There is no travel charge and nothing added for the distance. We are in Greene County most weeks.
