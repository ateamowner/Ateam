A Bit of Fairborn

The Town That Moved Itself

Fairborn is two villages that became one. Fairfield was founded in 1816, Osborn in 1850. After the 1913 flood the Huffman Dam went in, and it put Osborn inside the new flood pool — so from 1920 the village moved. Around two hundred houses and as many outbuildings were jacked up, hauled across by mule and tractor, and set back down on new foundations. The two merged in 1950, taking half a name each.

Then the base did the rest. Between 1950 and 1970 the town grew to roughly six times its size on Wright-Patterson, which is why so much of Fairborn is one twenty-year cohort of houses and concrete, all reaching the same age together.

It is a genuinely unusual exterior job for that reason: pre-1920 buildings and 1960s tract housing in the same day's work, wanting completely different treatment.
