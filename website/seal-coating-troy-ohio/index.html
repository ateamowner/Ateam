Local Knowledge

Why Troy's Newer Concrete Needs This Now

Troy's building boom off Peters Road, Barnhart Road and Stonebridge in the 1990s and 2000s means a lot of this city's driveway concrete just crossed the twenty-to-thirty-year mark — old enough for surface wear to start showing, young enough that sealing now actually saves it.

Concrete has a lifecycle, and sealing extends it

Unsealed concrete absorbs water every time it rains or snows. Over enough winters, that water freezing and expanding inside the pores is what causes the pitting, flaking and hairline cracking you start to notice on a twenty-year-old driveway. A sealer doesn't undo existing damage, but it stops feeding it.

The historic core is a different job

We treat Troy's older brick homes near West Main and the Public Square differently for cleaning — soft wash, masonry-safe detergent — and the same logic carries over to sealing: brick and older mortar aren't candidates for the same acrylic concrete sealer, so we scope that separately if it comes up.

Sealing doesn't fix a driveway that's already cracked

If your concrete already has active cracking or heaving, sealing slows further water damage but won't repair what's already broken. We'll tell you straight if a driveway is past the point where sealing alone is the right call.
