The short answer

Soft washing is the method the shingle industry itself describes for removing algae. What the manufacturers warn against is pressure washing, scrubbing, and using chemicals they haven't approved. But warranties are written by each manufacturer for each product line. We can't promise what your specific warranty says, so the right move is to read it or call the maker. That takes ten minutes and settles it.

What GAF and ARMA actually say

ARMA, the asphalt roofing manufacturers' trade group

The Asphalt Roofing Manufacturers Association says algae discoloration "may be lightened by applying a solution of liquid household bleach (sodium hypochlorite) and water," that the solution "should be gently dispersed on the roofing surface," and that "other cleaning chemicals or methods should not be used without the approval of the shingle manufacturer." ARMA also encourages using professionals, because the process makes the roof slippery.

Then, in capital letters: "DO NOT use a power washer or any type of brush or broom to clean algae from the roof surface." ARMA says high-pressure washing is likely to damage asphalt roofing.

GAF, a major shingle maker

GAF's technical bulletin on algae staining (TAB-R-102) recommends a mix of water, bleach and trisodium phosphate (or a phosphate-free substitute), applied, left to sit for up to 20 minutes, and rinsed with low-pressure water, with shrubs protected below. Under "What NOT to do," it says not to power wash shingles because it "may dislodge granules, which can lead to premature shingle failure."

That's the core of it. Both point to a low-pressure, bleach-based clean. Both say no pressure washer.

Why pressure washing is the real risk

Asphalt shingles are covered in mineral granules. They shield the asphalt from UV and weather. High pressure strips them off. The roof looks clean that afternoon and then ages early, and loose granules end up in your gutters.

So if someone offers to "power wash" your roof, that's the method the manufacturers specifically warn against. Soft wash for roofs and siding. Pressure wash for concrete. If you only remember one rule, that's the one.

How to check your own warranty

  1. Find the paperwork. Your roofer should have given you the manufacturer's limited warranty and, for some products, a registration.
  2. Look for a maintenance or cleaning section. Note any approved cleaning method or chemicals, and any exclusions for "improper maintenance."
  3. If it's unclear, call the manufacturer. Ask: "Is a low-pressure sodium hypochlorite soft wash, rinsed with low-pressure water, acceptable on my shingles?"
  4. Ask your cleaner what they use. A real roof cleaner will tell you the chemical, the dilution approach, and how they rinse. We do.

Algae-resistant shingles have their own warranty

Some newer shingles carry a separate algae warranty. GAF, for example, offers StainGuard algae protection limited warranties on qualifying products sold in packages bearing the StainGuard logos. Under that warranty, if covered shingles show pronounced blue-green algae discoloration during the coverage period, GAF's remedy can be the reasonable cost of commercially cleaning them. If your roof is newer and already streaking, check whether that coverage applies before paying for a cleaning.
