Leaf Stains on Your Driveway or Patio: Why They Happen and What Removes Them

Those brown leaf prints are tannins, not dirt. Here's how to get them out and keep them out.

The short answer

Leaf stains are tannins, the same plant compounds that color tea. A wet leaf sitting on concrete for a few days leaches them into the surface. Fresh marks often lift with a sweep and a rinse. Set-in stains spread across a drive or patio come out evenly with a pressure wash and the right pre-treatment. Clear leaves off before they sit through a rain and most stains never form.

What leaf stains actually are

Leaves contain tannins, the same natural compounds that color tea. When a wet leaf sits on concrete or pavers, the tannins leach out and soak into the pores of the surface. That's why the stain is shaped like the leaf, and why it shows up worst where leaves sat wet for days: under the maple, along the edge of the patio, in the low spot by the garage.

It isn't dirt and it isn't algae. That matters, because the fix is different.

Plenty of driveways have all three. We look at what's on the slab before we pick a pre-treatment, because rust needs a rust-specific treatment, not more pressure.

What you can do yourself

  • Get leaves off fast. Most tannin staining needs a few damp days to set. Blowing the drive off after a windy day or before a rain prevents most of it.
  • Rinse fresh marks. A new, light stain often fades with a sweep, a garden-hose rinse and a few sunny days.
  • Use an oxygen-based cleaner on set-in spots. Tannins respond to oxygen-based (non-chlorine) cleaners with some dwell time. Follow the label and keep it off your plants.
  • Skip the wand tip. A rented pressure washer with a narrow tip chases one stain at a time and leaves stripes and etched lines in the concrete. That damage is permanent.

When a pressure wash makes sense

If the stains are scattered across the whole driveway or patio, spot treating turns into a patchwork. That's when a professional wash pays off. Concrete gets pressure washed. We pre-treat, then run a rotating surface cleaner that holds the same distance across the whole slab, so the finish comes out even instead of striped. Then we detail the edges and rinse.

One honest note: tannins that have baked into old, porous concrete for several seasons may lighten a lot rather than vanish completely. We'll tell you that before we start, not after.

Timing it with Dayton leaf pickup

The City of Dayton's loose-leaf curbside collection runs from Oct. 19, 2026 through Jan. 4, 2027, with at least two passes per neighborhood. Leaves need to be at the curb by 8 a.m. Monday of your pickup week, and bagged leaves aren't collected.

That makes a simple plan: get the leaves off the concrete and to the curb, then wash once the trees over the drive are bare. Washing before the last drop means new leaves land on clean concrete and start the cycle again.
