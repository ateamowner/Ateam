Best Time of Year to Pressure Wash a House in Ohio

Two windows a year get you the most out of the money — and one common mistake has people paying to clean the same house twice in a spring.

By Anthony Leonard, A-Team Contracting · August 11, 2026 · Tipp City, Ohio

You can pressure wash an Ohio house any time the weather cooperates. But there are two windows a year where the work does the most good for the money, and one common mistake that has people paying to clean a house twice.

The season, in plain terms

Exterior cleaning in the Miami Valley runs from roughly April through November. The limit isn't comfort, it's chemistry and safety: detergents lose effectiveness as temperatures drop, and below about 40°F you're creating ice on the surfaces you just cleaned and on whatever your crew is standing on. We won't book work into a hard freeze, and you shouldn't hire anyone who will.

Inside that window there are two clear best times.

Late spring — mid-May through June

This is the biggest booking window of the year, for good reason. Winter leaves a real mess: road salt spray on lower siding, months of accumulated grime, and algae that had a damp, dark season to establish itself. Washing in late spring resets everything and buys you a clean house through the entire outdoor season.

The mistake to avoid: going too early. Book the first warm week of April and you'll have a beautifully clean house directly in the path of three weeks of tree pollen. In this part of Ohio the heavy pollen is generally done by mid-May. Wait for it.

Early fall — September through mid-October

The underrated one. A full Ohio summer of heat and humidity is exactly what algae wants, so by September the north side of most houses has visible growth. Washing it off now means it doesn't spend the entire winter sitting damp against your siding, and it means your house looks right through the holidays.

Fall is also easier to schedule. Everyone books in May; almost nobody books in September, which usually means you get the date you actually want.

If you're selling

Wash two weeks before the photographer comes, not two days. Freshly cleaned concrete stays visibly darker while it holds water and can take four or five days to come back to its real color — which means a driveway shot the morning after a wash can photograph worse than it did dirty. Two weeks is the safe margin, and it's the single most common timing mistake we see from sellers.

How often, realistically

Most Ohio homes want a house wash once a year. Shaded lots, tree-heavy streets and properties near the Great Miami or Mad River green up faster and shouldn't stretch it. Open, sunny lots can often go 18 months. Roofs are a different cycle entirely — a proper soft wash should hold for four to six years, and if yours is streaking again within a year, it was pressure washed rather than treated.

The scheduling reality

April through June is our busiest stretch and it books out weeks in advance. If you want a specific week in spring, get on the calendar in March. If you're flexible, September is the best-value month of the year — same work, same price, far more available dates.
