Gutters Overflowing in Heavy Rain? Why It Happens After Leaf Drop, and How to Fix a Clogged Downspout

Why gutters overflow in heavy rain

Almost every overflow call this time of year comes down to one of four things.

A packed trough

Wet leaves, seed pods and shingle grit settle to the bottom of the gutter and turn into sludge. From the ground the gutter can look fine. Up close, the trough is half full, and when the rain comes it has nowhere to go but over the front edge.

A clogged outlet or downspout elbow

Leaves mat over the outlet hole where the gutter drains into the downspout, or they jam at the first bend. The water backs up and spills at the corner. When we flush a downspout that won't drain, it's usually packed at the elbow.

A downspout that drains into the ground

Some downspouts run into a buried pipe instead of onto a splash block. If that line is plugged, water backs up the downspout even when the gutter itself is clean. You'll often see water bubbling up where the downspout meets the ground.

Gutters that are clean and still overflow

Water runs fast off big roof sections and out of valleys. A gutter that doesn't slope toward the downspout, or doesn't have enough downspouts, can't keep up. InterNACHI says gutters should slope toward the downspout at 1/16 inch per foot, and that areas with heavy rainfall need more gutter and downspout capacity. That's an installer's job, not a cleaning. What to check from the ground during a storm

Stay off the ladder in the rain and wind. Everything below you can see from the yard or a window.

  • Water sheeting over the front edge mid-run: the trough is packed.
  • Water spilling at one corner, next to a downspout: the outlet or elbow is clogged.
  • Little or no water coming out the bottom of the downspout while the gutter overflows: the downspout is clogged.
  • Water bubbling up where the downspout meets the ground, or backing up out of a buried pipe: the underground line may be the problem.
  • Plants or seedlings growing out of the gutter: the debris has been sitting wet for a long time.
  • A gutter sagging or pulled away in one spot: wet debris is heavy, and the hardware may be loose. Take a photo.
  • Water pouring behind the gutter, between the gutter and the fascia: note where. We'll look at it on the cleaning visit. Repair is a separate job.

Then look at where the water lands. InterNACHI says downspouts should send water 4 to 6 feet away from the foundation. If yours dumps right at the wall, the soil there stays soaked.

What overflow does to your house

Siding. Overflow keeps the wall below it wet, and wet walls grow green algae stripes under the gutter line. If you already see those, house washing in Dayton takes them off with a soft wash, not a pressure wand.

Fascia and soffits. Water that spills over the back of the gutter soaks the wood behind it. I covered that in why the fall cleaning matters most.

Foundation and basement. InterNACHI says a few inches of rain on a roof can produce several thousand gallons of runoff, and if that water isn't carried away it can saturate the soil and wick through the foundation. Clear gutters and downspouts help carry that water away from the house.

Winter. Gutters that go into winter full fill with ice at the eave. For what does and doesn't cause ice dams, see ice dams, explained straight.

How to fix a clogged downspout (what's safe to try yourself)

From the ground. If the bottom extension or elbow slides off, pull it, clear what's in it by hand, and run a garden hose up from the bottom. On a single-story house you can also run the hose down from the top. InterNACHI suggests flushing gutters and downspouts with a hose and keeping that water directed away from the house.

On a ladder. A single-story house on a dry day is a reasonable DIY job with a steady ladder, gloves and a gutter scoop. Keep three points of contact on the ladder, and move the ladder instead of reaching.

When to hand it off. Two stories, gutter guards, or a downspout that runs underground. That's where hiring someone pays off.

Skip the chemical drain cleaners, and don't run a pressure washer up a downspout. Water and your hands do the job.

Why a blower-only cleaning sets up the next clog

If you paid for a quick blow-out this fall and you're still overflowing, this is probably why. A blower pushes the debris around, and a lot of it ends up packed into the downspouts. That's what causes the next clog.
