Listing Prep Exterior Cleaning for Dayton Realtors

Soft wash siding and roofs. Pressure wash the drive. Clear the glass — before photo day.

Buyers decide from the curb before they open the door. Algae on the north wall, a black-streaked roof, a zebra-striped driveway, and filmed windows all show up in the listing photos — and they stick in the showing notes.

A Team Contracting runs listing-prep exterior cleaning for Dayton-metro agents and sellers: soft wash for siding and roofs, pressure / concrete wash for driveways and sidewalks, streak-free windows for photo day. Method matched to the surface. Owner on site. One ticket, timed to your list date.

This is not a DIY guide. It’s the agent checklist we use when a listing needs to look sharp before the camera truck rolls.

Why listing photos punish a dirty exterior

MLS and Zillow crop tight on the front elevation. Green film on vinyl, mildew in the shade band, and roof streaks read as “deferred maintenance” even when the house is solid. A driveway with algae and wand marks does the same for the approach.

Buyers don’t need a facility report to notice. They need one scroll. Soft wash removes algae on siding and roofs. Soft wash now — before winter locks that stain in — if you’re listing into fall or early winter. Concrete still gets pressure wash. Wrong method on the wrong surface shows up in the close-ups.

The listing-prep split (don’t let one-method crews “save time”)

Hard rule on every A Team ticket:

  • Siding and roofs → soft wash (low pressure + detergent that kills algae at the root)
  • Driveways, sidewalks, patios → pressure wash / concrete wash (force + surface cleaner)
  • Windows → exterior glass finished for photo day
  • Never soft-wash language on concrete. Never high pressure on vinyl or asphalt shingles.

A one-line “pressure wash the whole house” scope is how siding gets hurt and algae comes back on the elevations you just photographed. Split the scope on purpose. Full method chart: Soft Wash vs Pressure Wash · Soft Washing vs. Pressure Washing.

Agent checklist: what to book before photos

1. Soft wash the elevations (and the roof if it’s streaking)

North walls in the Miami Valley go green first — shade plus humidity from the Great Miami and Mad River valleys. Soft wash lifts algae and mildew without driving water under the laps. Black roof streaks (Gloeocapsa magma) are soft wash only. High pressure strips UV granules and shortens roof life.

House wash detail for Dayton stock: House washing in Dayton. Fall timing for algae on siding/roof: Soft wash before winter.

2. Pressure wash the driveway, walks, and apron

Concrete needs a surface cleaner — not a freehand wand that leaves zebra stripes in the listing hero shot. Algae on walks is slick and shows as neglect in the approach photos. Same visit as the house wash is normal; two methods, one crew.

3. Windows for photo day

Streak-free exterior glass is the cheapest “make the whole house look sharper” add. Buyers and photographers notice filmed storms and upstairs panes. Bundle windows with the wash while we’re already on site. Window detail: window cleaning.

4. Timing relative to list date

Ideal window: finish exterior wash 2–5 days before professional photos so the surfaces dry, beds look reset, and nothing has had time to re-film. If frost is in the forecast, soft wash while detergents still work — soft wash removes algae; soft wash now — before winter locks that stain in. Don’t wait for a May listing if the north wall is already green in October.

How a listing-prep visit runs

  1. Scope by surface — elevations soft wash; hardscape pressure; windows if on the ticket. We confirm on site before we start.
  2. Protect — plants pre-wet and covered; fixtures and openings checked.
  3. Soft wash / dwell / rinse — chemistry on siding and roof; controlled rinse top down.
  4. Pressure wash flatwork — surface cleaner on drive and walks; no wand tiger stripes for the camera.
  5. Windows — exterior glass finished when bundled.
  6. Proof — before-and-after so you can show the seller (and the broker) what changed from the street.

Seller doesn’t have to babysit the job. Spigot access and the surfaces we’re treating are enough. We’re based in Tipp City and cover Dayton, Huber Heights, Fairborn, Beavercreek, Vandalia, Troy, Englewood, Kettering, Centerville, and the Miami Valley towns agents actually list in.

What we will not do on a listing ticket

  • High-pressure blast on vinyl, painted wood, or asphalt shingles “to go faster”
  • Soft-wash detergent marketed as a driveway clean (wrong surface, wrong language)
  • Invented package prices in chat — ballpark online, price confirmed on site before work starts
  • Private street names or neighbor addresses in public copy

If the house needs paint more than wash (chalking, peeling clapboard), we’ll say so. A soft wash won’t fix failed paint — and listing photos will show it either way.

For sellers (short)

You’re not hiring a marketing department. You’re hiring a crew that matches method to surface so the photos don’t advertise last summer’s algae. Soft wash the house and roof. Pressure wash the concrete. Clear the glass. Then shoot.
