Local Knowledge

Dayton Housing Isn't One Kind of Job

Downtown high-rises and rowhouses, century-old brick in South Park and the Oregon District, mid-century ranches further out, newer builds near the university — Dayton doesn't have one housing type, so it doesn't get one wash. We look at the surface first and pick the method that fits it, not the other way around.

Concrete gets pressure washed. Brick and siding get a soft wash.

A driveway, sidewalk or patio slab can take real pressure — that's the actual pressure-washing job, and it's what most of a Dayton exterior visit is. Brick, stone, painted siding and anything with old mortar is a different story. High pressure on a wall that's stood since before 1950 chews out the joint face and forces water where it doesn't belong. That work gets a low-pressure soft wash instead — same clean result, no damage.

Rentals, turnovers and multi-family

A lot of Dayton's housing is rental stock, and a lot of our Dayton calls are landlords and property managers — turnover cleaning between tenants, or a recurring exterior schedule on a multi-family building. See commercial pressure washing for how we set that up.

Where we work

All of it — downtown, South Park, the Oregon District, Wright-Dunbar, Belmont, near the University of Dayton, and the neighborhoods between. Dayton is about sixteen miles down I-75 from our shop in Tipp City, roughly a twenty-minute drive, and we're through the city most weeks — no travel charge on a Dayton address.

Already have a house wash quote?

If you're set on house washing already, add the driveway and walks to the same visit. Setup and travel are the expensive parts of a small job — once we're there, flatwork is cheap to add on.
