Know Before You Book

Soft Washing vs.
Pressure Washing

Two completely different processes that people use interchangeably — and picking the wrong one is how siding gets water behind it and roofs lose their granules. Here's which surface takes which.

The short answer: soft washing cleans with a biodegradable detergent at low pressure — roughly 100–500 PSI, about garden-hose force — and the chemistry kills the algae at the root. Pressure washing cleans with high-force water at 1,500–4,000 PSI and relies on that force alone. Hard flatwork like concrete and pavers gets pressure washed. Siding, roofs, stucco and painted wood get soft washed.

Why "it came back in a month" happens

This is the single most common complaint we hear about a previous contractor, and it has one cause. Green algae and the black staining on roofs (Gloeocapsa magma) are living organisms with structures that reach into the pores of the surface they're growing on. High-pressure water removes the visible layer on top. It does not remove what's below, and what's below regrows — fast, in an Ohio summer.

Soft washing applies a solution that kills the organism outright and gives it time to work before rinsing. It looks less dramatic while it's happening. It's the reason a soft-washed house stays clean through a season instead of greening up by August.

How to vet a contractor in two questions

1. "What PSI will you put on my siding?" — anything over about 500 is the wrong answer. 2. "Do you use a detergent, or just water?" — "just water, we turn the pressure up" means the algae will be back before fall. Two questions, thirty seconds, and you'll know.

Soft Washing vs. Pressure Washing FAQ

What people ask us once they realize these are two different things.

Neither is better — they're for different surfaces. Pressure washing is right for hard, non-porous flatwork: concrete, brick pavers, stone. Soft washing is right for anything that can be damaged by force: siding, roofs, stucco, painted wood, screens. A company that only owns one method will use it on everything, and that's where damage comes from.
