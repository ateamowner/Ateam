Vandalia Has a Grime Problem Most Towns Don't

Living at the intersection of two interstates and an international airport has a cost, and part of it lands on the north and west walls of your house.

Most exterior cleaning in the Miami Valley is a biology problem: green algae and black Gloeocapsa magma feeding on moisture and shade. Vandalia has that too. But it also has a chemistry problem. I-70 and I-75 cross right here, US-40 runs through town, and Dayton International sits on the north edge. The traffic throws a continuous haze of tire rubber, brake dust and unburned diesel into the air, and it settles as a fine gray film on whatever faces the road.

That film is oily. Rinse it with plain water at any pressure and you mostly relocate it — which is why so many Vandalia homeowners tell us the house "never really came clean" the last time. It needs a surfactant that emulsifies the oil so the rinse can carry it off. Same visit, same crew, different chemistry.

The 1960s and 70s ranch belt

Cassel Hills, Sandalwood, Poplar Hill and the streets off Helke Road are full of original aluminum siding. Sixty years on, the factory paint film has chalked — a powdery oxide layer you can wipe off with a finger. High pressure drives that chalk around and leaves permanent streaking that no second wash will fix. Every aluminum house we touch gets soft washed, no exceptions.

Driveways in the flight path

Long approach drives are common here, and they collect three things at once: shade algae along the edges, road film across the middle, and oil where the cars park. Each one wants a different pre-treatment. We do them in order — degreaser on the oil spots first, detergent across the slab, then the surface cleaner for an even finish with no zebra stripes.

Selling a Vandalia house?

Wash two weeks before listing photos, not two days. Concrete darkens while it's damp and can take four or five days to return to its true dry color, and a photographer shooting a still-wet driveway gets a picture that looks worse than the before. Two weeks is the safe window.
