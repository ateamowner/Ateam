Local Knowledge

A City With No Old Town

Beavercreek is unusual among the places we work: it has no historic core at all. It was a township until 1980, and what is here now is farmland that turned into subdivisions, decade by decade, for fifty years.

Most towns are laid out in rings — old in the middle, newer as you go out. Beavercreek is laid out in patches. A 1960s street can sit next to a 2005 development which backs onto something built last year, and across twenty-seven square miles there is no reliable way to guess a house's age from where it stands.

That matters because the siding tells you what the map does not, and each generation of it behaves differently:

The postwar and 1960s streets

The oldest of it, put up as Wright-Patterson pulled people into the county. Greene County went from under 36,000 people in 1940 to nearly 95,000 by 1960, and a lot of that landed here. Expect early aluminum that chalks as it oxidizes, and painted wood trim. Pressure grinds that chalk into streaks that never come out.

The 1980s and 90s expansion

The big growth years, out along the I-675 and Fairfield Road corridors. This is thinner vinyl that has now had thirty-plus years of UV on it and lost some of its flex. It is the vintage most likely to be damaged by force, and the one homeowners most often assume is indestructible.

The 2000s and newer

Bigger houses, more elevation, and often three or four materials on one wall — vinyl, stone veneer, fibre cement, shakes in the gables. More surface area, more reach, and a mixed elevation that needs the solution matched to each material rather than one pass over everything.

What It Actually Is

Chemistry, Not Force

Soft washing runs at roughly garden-hose pressure — about 100 to 500 PSI at the wall, against 1,500 to 4,000 for pressure washing. A biodegradable detergent does the cleaning, dwells, then gets rinsed. The water only carries it on and takes it off.

The difference shows months later. What grows on siding is alive, with a root structure down in the pores. Blast the top off and the wall is clean that afternoon and green again by spring. Kill the organism and the clock genuinely resets.

It is also the only method that is safe across all three Beavercreek generations at once, which matters when the same street has 1965 aluminum on one house and 2015 fibre cement on the next.
