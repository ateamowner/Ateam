# Competitor Intel — Window Cleaning / Pressure Washing / Soft Washing / Roof Cleaning
### Tipp City · Troy · Dayton, Ohio market

**Prepared for:** A Team Contracting (ateamcontractings.com)
**Research date:** 2026-09-02
**Method:** Live web search (WebSearch) for Dayton-area service + city terms, followed by direct page fetches (WebFetch) of each competitor's homepage or Dayton-area landing page.

> **Verification standard used in this document**
> - **Observed** = fetched directly this session (or read in a search-result snippet, labeled as such) and the text/element was actually present.
> - **Not verified** = could not confirm. This is *not* a claim the thing is absent — only that it wasn't seen.
> - No review counts, prices, or rankings are estimated anywhere below — every number is what the page itself displayed.
> - H1s and CTA text were captured via automated page-fetch and are reported as close transcriptions, not guaranteed character-for-character OCR of the live DOM.
> - One target (Fish Window Cleaning) returned HTTP 404 on direct fetch (twice, on two different attempts); its row is built from WebSearch snippet text only and flagged accordingly.

## Per-competitor notes

### 1. Central Window Cleaning — centralexpertservices.com
Local company, 20+ years, based in Vandalia. H1 on the Dayton branch page: "Window Cleaning & Gutter Cleaning Service." CTA: "GET A QUOTE" (plus a direct phone number). Services: residential/commercial window cleaning, gutter cleaning, glass restoration, soft-wash roof and house cleaning, concrete/driveway pressure washing, deck/patio cleaning, storefronts. **Standout: 26 separate city landing pages** (17 in Montgomery County, 9 in Greene County) rather than one page listing cities — this is the strongest local-SEO page structure observed in this set. **3,000+ five-star Google reviews** badge and "HomeAdvisor Top Rating" are the biggest trust-signal numbers seen anywhere in this research. No pricing, no before/after gallery seen, no online booking beyond a quote form.

### 2. Two Dudes Window Cleaning — twodudeswc.com
Local, homepage H1: "Bringing High Definition To Your Windows." Structured as a location picker rather than a single sales page — visitor chooses "Dayton area" or "Mason area," each with its own long city list (Dayton area list includes Troy, Tipp City, and Vandalia — direct overlap with A Team's territory). A `/guarantee` page is linked in navigation but its terms weren't visible on the fetched page. No prices, no review count, no galleries observed on this page.

### 3. Stealth Pro Wash LLC — stealthprowash.com
Local, homepage H1: "Welcome To Stealth Pro Wash." CTA: "Request A Quote." Very long service list (16 items) including solar panel cleaning, brick cleaning, HOA/apartment-complex/building cleaning — broader than A Team's list. Displays a "SoftWash Systems Certified" badge. States "Satisfaction Guaranteed" / "we guarantee our work" without specific terms. No prices, no review count, no galleries, no crew photos observed.

### 4. Cleantec Softwash (Cleantec Ltd.) — cleantecsoftwash.com
Local, based in Eaton, OH. Dayton-area page H1: "Softwashing & Pressure Washing Services in Dayton, OH." CTA: "Request an Estimate." Displays a "Christian Blue Certified Business" badge alongside a BBB logo — a values-based affinity trust signal not seen elsewhere in this set. States a **"Money-back satisfaction guarantee"** — simpler and more universally legible than A Team's condition-specific guarantees. Has a "Recent Dayton Softwashing Gallery" section (before/after). Service area list is unusually wide, reaching into Indiana (Richmond, Union City).

### 5. Classy Window Cleaning — classywindowcleaning.com
Local, "over 30 years" in business, physical location in Beavercreek (per earlier search result: 1601 Grange Hall Rd). Homepage H1 on the fetched page skewed commercial: "State of Ohio Commercial High Rise Window Cleaning Services." CTA: "SCHEDULE WINDOW CLEANING SERVICE," backed by a **vcita online calendar scheduler** — the only true self-service booking calendar (not just a contact form) observed in this set. Long residential add-on list: chandelier cleaning, ceiling fan cleaning, high dusting/bulb replacement, Leaf Guard gutter-guard installation. Named customer quotes (Jeffrey D., Ada B., Michele N.) but no aggregate review count.

### 6. OutShine Window Cleaning — outshinewc.com
Operates across Ohio, Texas, Kentucky, Indiana, and North Carolina — a multi-market operator; whether it is structured as a franchise or a single multi-location company was not determined this session. Dayton page H1: "Commercial Window Cleaning in Dayton, OH." CTA: "Get a Quote," with a dedicated `/quote/dayton/` form URL. **Explicitly markets weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, and quarterly recurring commercial cleaning as selectable cadence options** — the clearest recurring-revenue merchandising seen in this research. Header states "Fully Insured · No Contracts." Names and photographs a Service Manager and a Sales Manager (not just a founder/owner) — a trust tactic no one else in this set used.

## Gap list — concrete, actionable differences vs. ateamcontractings.com

**G1. Review-count depth is A Team's single biggest visible trust gap.**
Central Window Cleaning shows 3,000+ five-star Google reviews; Apply Pressure Washing shows 4.9★/250+ reviews; Redhead shows 5.0★/55 reviews. A Team shows 5.0★/32 reviews. A Team's rating is as strong as anyone's, but the *count* reads small next to Central's in particular. Action: run an active review-generation push (post-job text/email ask) — 32 is a number that will keep losing the credibility comparison to any competitor who has been asking for reviews longer.

**G2. City-page density and city×service combination pages.**
Central Window Cleaning runs 26 separate, individually-URLed city landing pages; OutShine has a dedicated `/market/dayton/` plus a separate `/quote/dayton/`; Redhead and Cleantec both have their own Dayton-specific pages. A Team confirmed only that `/troy-ohio` exists as a full page; coverage for the other 8 listed cities (Vandalia, Huber Heights, Piqua, Fairborn, Beavercreek, Englewood, Dayton, Tipp City) was not checked and no city×service page (e.g., "window cleaning in Beavercreek") was found. Action: audit whether all 9 cities have a page as complete as Troy's, and consider city×service pages for the highest-value combinations (e.g. roof cleaning + Beavercreek, window cleaning + Dayton).

**G3. No standalone single-service page was found for A Team.**
Two guessed URLs for a dedicated window-cleaning page both 404'd. Every competitor fetched routes service-specific search intent to either a service page or at minimum a service-labeled section. Action: confirm whether A Team has indexable single-service pages (e.g. `/window-cleaning`, `/roof-cleaning`) at all, under some slug; if not, this is a clear content gap versus a market where most players have them.

**G4. Recurring/cadence merchandising for commercial customers.**
OutShine explicitly sells weekly, bi-weekly, monthly, and quarterly recurring commercial window cleaning as selectable options. A Team's "Clean Club" is residential-only, with exactly two fixed choices (2×/year windows, or an annual house+driveway bundle). Action: if A Team does any repeat commercial work, merchandise a commercial recurring-cadence menu the way OutShine does — this is a clean, low-cost content addition, not a new service line.

**G5. Dollar-and-time-denominated guarantees, marketed as headline copy.**
Apply Pressure Washing headlines a 3-year roof warranty, a 1-year house-wash warranty, and a named price/value guarantee ("refund the difference plus give you $50"). A Team's actual guarantees (12-month algae re-clean, 24-month roof-streak guarantee) are comparable or longer in duration but aren't framed with the same dollar-back specificity. Action: keep the existing terms, but consider adding one dollar-denominated guarantee (a price-match promise, for instance) to match the persuasive framing competitors are using on materially similar terms.

**G6. Certification badges beyond BBB.**
Apply Pressure Washing names "SoftWash Systems Certified" and "PWNA certification" for its technicians and states a "$1 million general liability policy" outright. Stealth Pro Wash also displays a SoftWash Systems Certified badge. A Team shows a BBB A+ badge and confirms insurance only in FAQ text, with no certification badge or stated coverage amount. Action: if any A Team crew hold SoftWash Systems, PWNA, or similar certifications, display the badge; if not, evaluate getting certified — it's a differentiator three separate competitors in this set are already using, and stating the liability coverage figure is a one-line copy change.

**G7. Named, titled staff beyond the owner.**
OutShine names and photographs a Service Manager and a Sales Manager with job titles, not just a founder photo. A Team shows owner Anthony Leonard but it wasn't established whether any other crew are individually named/titled on the site. Action: if A Team has employees beyond the owner, name and photograph them with roles — "you'll know exactly who's on your property" is a trust angle only one competitor in this set is using, and A Team already has the raw material (a real owner-forward site) to extend it.

**G8. Long-tail named services for SEO capture.**
Redhead Pressure Cleaning individually names 20 services, including graffiti removal, rust/oil stain removal, pool deck cleaning, dumpster pad cleaning, and paver cleaning/sealing — none of which appear as named line items in A Team's observed service list (which groups these under broader categories like "concrete cleaning"). Action: if A Team already performs any of these, give them their own named mentions (even a line in an FAQ or service list) purely to catch long-tail search terms Redhead is currently positioned for and A Team is not.

**G9. A simple, unconditional "money-back" guarantee headline.**
Cleantec Softwash markets a flat money-back satisfaction guarantee — one sentence, no conditions to read. A Team's guarantees are more generous in substance (12/24-month terms) but require reading the specific condition (algae, organic streaking) to understand what's covered. Action: consider pairing the existing specific guarantees with one plain-language "satisfaction guaranteed, full stop" headline for the customer who won't read past a T-shirt-length promise.

**G10. Self-service online booking calendar.**
Classy Window Cleaning is the only competitor observed using a real scheduling calendar (vcita) rather than a lead-capture form. A Team's estimator returns a price but still ends in "contact us to confirm" rather than a bookable time slot. This is a smaller gap since A Team's estimator is otherwise the most functionally advanced quoting tool observed in the set (see below) — flagged as a lower-priority, nice-to-have addition, not a competitive deficiency.

## Where A Team already leads this set (for balance, not a gap)

- **Instant photo-based price estimator returning real, itemized numbers.** No competitor fetched in this research offers anything comparable — most tools observed are plain lead-capture forms ("Get a Quote," "Request an Estimate").
- **CTA specificity.** "SNAP A PIC. GET A PRICE →" is a concrete action + concrete outcome; every competitor's primary CTA observed was a generic "Get a Quote" / "Request a Quote" / "Get Pricing" variant.
- **Named, photographed owner with real work photos.** Only OutShine (staff, not owner) came close; most competitors in this set show no personal photos at all.
- **A recurring membership product at all.** "Clean Club" has real terms (10% off, defined cadence); no local competitor in this set was observed offering any residential membership program — OutShine's cadence options are commercial-only.
