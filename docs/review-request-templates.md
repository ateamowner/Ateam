# Review request templates

Local Falcon's recommendation #5 was to build a steady review flow, with
customers naturally mentioning **the service performed and the town**. That
wording matters: Google reads review text, and reviews that say "pressure
washing in Tipp City" reinforce the exact terms you're trying to rank for in a
way that a bare five stars does not.

**Your review link:**
https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=ChIJ9WZxhfcBzYoR2gQ4SyHDlVQ

Copy it straight from here into the text or email below. Shorten it with your
Bitly account if you want something readable on a printed invoice or yard sign.

**This link is deliberately not on the website.** Site visitors are prospects —
they haven't hired you yet, so they can't review you, and a "leave a review"
button sitting next to "Get My Instant Price" only competes with the thing you
actually want them to do. Review requests belong in the 1:1 ask below, where
they're aimed at someone who just watched you finish the job. The homepage does
link out to *read* your Google reviews, which is social proof pointed at
prospects — a different job entirely.

---

## When to ask

Ask **at the walkthrough**, while you're still standing in front of the finished
work and they're reacting to it. That's the moment with the highest yes rate.
Then send the text the same evening — the in-person ask is what makes the text
land, and the text is what makes it actually happen.

Don't batch-send to old customers all at once. A sudden burst of reviews after
months of silence looks unnatural. A steady trickle is worth more.

---

## Text message — send same day

> Hey [Name], Anthony with A Team. Really glad the [driveway] turned out well
> today. If you've got 30 seconds, would you mind leaving us a quick Google
> review? It genuinely helps a small local business like ours get found.
>
> [link]
>
> If you can mention the [pressure washing] and that you're in [Tipp City],
> that helps folks nearby find us too. Thanks!

Swap the bracketed parts every time. The service and the town are the whole
point of the message.

## Rotate the service wording

You now have six service pages, and reviews that echo their language help those
pages specifically. Rotate naturally based on the job:

| Job done | Ask them to mention |
|---|---|
| Driveway / patio / walkway | "concrete cleaning" or "pressure washing" |
| Siding, eaves, stucco | "soft washing" |
| Roof, black streaks, moss | "roof cleaning" |
| Gutters | "gutter cleaning" |
| Windows | "exterior window cleaning" |

And rotate the town to match the actual job — Tipp City, Troy, Vandalia,
Huber Heights, Dayton, Piqua, Beavercreek, Fairborn. Reviews spread across your
real service area are more convincing than 30 that all say Tipp City.

## What not to do

- **Don't offer discounts or anything of value for a review.** It violates
  Google's policies and can get reviews removed or the profile penalized.
- **Don't write them yourself or have staff post them.** Google is good at
  detecting this and the penalty is severe.
- **Don't gate them** — asking only happy customers for public reviews while
  routing unhappy ones to a private form is against Google's policy.
- **Don't paste review text onto the website as `aggregateRating` markup.**
  Reviews collected on Google are third-party; marking them up as your own
  aggregate rating risks a manual action. The site links to the real profile
  instead, which gives the same trust signal safely.

## Respond to every review

Reply to all of them, positive and negative — it's a ranking signal and it's
visible to everyone reading. For a negative one, respond calmly, factually, and
offer to fix it offline. Future customers read the reply more closely than the
complaint.
